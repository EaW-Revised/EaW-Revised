# Deliberate deviations from the original

Places where the remake intentionally differs from the original game. Each row names the evidence level of the
original behaviour; "unverified" means no capture or debug-build walk backs it.

| Id | Area | Original (evidence) | Remake | Reason | Issue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DEV-1 | Skirmish selection | Only own objects, plus allied community property in a multiplayer tactical game, can be selected (debug-build walk of the selection admission in the FoC debug build via GhidrAssist; no capture, no committed walk artifact). Whether skirmish against AI counts as that mode, and the panel contents, are unverified by capture. | A click on an allied structure inspects it: health card, no commands, build menu or abilities; viewer-local, no net message. Own and enemy objects unchanged. An allied click replaces your current selection with the inspect card; reselect your units afterwards. Allied build pads and community-property stations keep their existing shared control ((legacy EAWR-1571), WBP-08/09, WSU-16: selectable with build UI); DEV-1 covers the other allied structures. | Owner decision 2026-10-24: the mechanic never made sense. | (legacy EAWR-4919) |
| DEV-2 | Particle presentation: one catch-up pass where the difference is under half a pixel (D-ss) | Retail runs one particle update per rendered frame; emitters consume the elapsed time of the pose just supplied and births are placed from the emitter's motion over that interval (PS-42, PS-10; behaviour docs). | In a frame that owes two or more ticks, a ship whose emitters would differ by less than 0.5 px on screen (predicted from the two pose snapshots and the current camera; back at 0.25 px) takes one pass at the drawn pose instead of a historical pass plus the drawn pass; culled ships always take one. Births are placed along the straight chord between the last and the new pose, so the difference is the path's deviation from that chord. Simulation and per-tick hashes are identical. Pixels of fire and smoke clusters in crowded fleets differ: on the RaW60 build host pair at 2 ticks/frame 21 479 of 921 600 pixels (2.33%) of the final frame, mean 0.066, max channel 205, 34 pixels >= 128; the threshold bounds the origin path error, not this pixel difference. The owner watched a build host RaW60 off/on clip (2 ticks/frame, ticks 3400-3600, about 2.4% of pixels differ per frame, sim hashes identical) on 2026-11-01 at 09:05 and accepted it. Not covered: close-up fighter trails, other ticks per frame. | Removes most of the historical pass in frames below 30 fps. | (legacy EAWR-5594) |
| DEV-3 | Galactic route cost width | Retail stores the route cost in 16 bits, so it overflows at 65 000+ route units (evidence level as recorded on (legacy EAWR-4323, EAWR-4312); no new capture). | Routes are computed exactly with 64-bit cost (shipped in c6c16e11); no overflow. No replay or checkpoint change. The documented ceiling stays: galaxies of more than 1000 planets are still capped. | Owner decision 2026-11-01 (legacy EAWR-4323): keep the wider number. | (legacy EAWR-4323, EAWR-4312) |
| DEV-4 | Galactic hero bar: live and scheduled entry of one type | Retail appends a scheduled-spawn entry for every named local hero in a planet spawn list without checking the live scan, so a type present in both would take two slots (debug-build hero-bar walk via GhidrAssist, 2026-11-01; no capture of the overlap, which needs a hero both alive and scheduled). | One portrait per type: the live entry wins and the scheduled one is dropped. Presentation only; no sim, hash or replay change. | A named hero is unique, so the overlap is a transient oddity and a duplicate portrait has no value. | (legacy EAWR-3899) |
| DEV-5 | Galactic story player lookup in co-op | `Find_Player("local")` uses the local machine's player slot (WST-91; debug-build walk). Co-op on single-player campaign entries is not a retail mode. | In a networked campaign with single-player rules and one shared human faction, the lookup returns that agreed faction on every peer. Other networked campaigns do not expose a per-peer local player. | A per-machine value would diverge in lockstep; the shared faction lets co-op story scripts use a deterministic player. | (legacy EAWR-5626) |
| DEV-6 | Duplicate object names across layers | When two object definitions share a name, retail goes by the order of the object file lists: the first registered definition wins, whatever layer it came from, so a stock object listed before a mod's same-named one wins (retail exe capture on the rig, 2026-11-07, cases 1-4, plus the debug-build walk of the name registration). Within one layer retail also takes the first listed. | A mod's object always wins over a same-named stock object, regardless of list order (layer first, then registry order, then place in the file). Within one layer the first listed wins, as in the original. Affected today: two Remake mod versions, where retail shows the stock object for 28 and 15 names and the remake shows the mod's. Fact only, no promise: D-01 ranks a parent mod's files below stock. | Owner decision 2026-11-07: a mod overrides stock. | (legacy EAWR-5956) |
| DEV-7 | Tactical Lua build-pad requests | A second `GameObject.Build` on the same pad in one tick is refused and answers nil (debug build EXP-E01). | Calls that pass the completed-view checks answer true; ordered resolution admits the first start on each pad and drops later starts in that barrier (EX-60). | Owner decision: preserve deterministic results across simulation worker counts while scripts remain partitioned. | (legacy EAWR-5999, EAWR-5829) |
| DEV-8 | Galactic AI decision timing | The AI's world actions (production, fleet orders, invasions) and its bookkeeping (script variables, pay-as-you-go billing decisions, story-plot commands) take effect as the AI performs its work (timing evidence unverified by capture). | The AI decides in windows of 15 ticks from the world as it was when the window began, plus the AI's own world actions from earlier windows that have not applied yet. Each decision's world action applies at its own apply tick, 30 to 44 ticks (one to one and a half seconds at normal speed) after that snapshot (the batch schedule of the async AI); each window owns fixed entity and invasion identity blocks reserved when it begins. World events for the AI (production outcomes, movement events, unit lifecycle changes, battle notices) reach it at its next window start, in the order they happened. That includes the story scripts' Spawn_Unit and Prevent_AI_Usage: the new object enters the world at once and its AI owner's free store at that window start, and a Prevent_AI_Usage reaches the AI's free store then too; the script's answer is decided from the world immediately: a selected AI owner is accepted even before its pool is initialized. The world retains both actor facts and seeds them into a later pool. Income, windfalls and pay-as-you-go bills reach its budget the same way; between windows a bill is decided against the budget the AI published at its last apply tick plus the income, windfalls and bills since (at most about 1 s old), and the AI books that recorded outcome without deciding again (a charge for a goal it has removed meanwhile goes to its dead budget). The story-plot edits of the AI's plan scripts (Get_Story_Plot) take effect at the window's apply tick, after that window's world actions; an edit the plot engine refuses is reported at that tick. Runtime notes the AI raises during a window (a failed battle notification, a retired AI provider, its plan scripts' own notes) are reported at that tick too. Script variables (GlobalValue) are split: story scripts read and write their own store at once; the AI's plan scripts see their own writes at once and the story's from their next window, and the AI's writes reach the story's store at the window's apply tick (on the same key the AI's later write wins). The AI's remaining bookkeeping is still written during the window; the remaining async-lane steps move it to the window's first apply tick. Human player actions are unaffected. | Owner approved 2026-11-04 (restated 2026-11-07) to let AI work run without blocking the simulation. | AI lane work (legacy EAWR-5806) |
| DEV-9 | Optional galactic radar fit | Galactic radar uses fixed centred half-extents of 380 X and 460 Y, even when authored planet positions extend beyond them (WGUI-90/142, debug-build walk). | Options > Tweaks > Fit radar to galaxy, off by default, expands only axes exceeded by loaded campaign planet positions, with a margin of 2.5% of the full span at each edge; planet markers, camera marker and clicks share the transform. The default retains retail extents. Presentation only. | Owner decision 2026-11-07: allow large mod galaxies to fit without a mod-specific rule (WGUI-177). | (legacy EAWR-5858, EAWR-5967) |
