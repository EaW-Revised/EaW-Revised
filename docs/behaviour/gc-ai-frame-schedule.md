# Galactic AI frame service schedule

## Async lane cut and adoption (GFS-A28)

The sim also owns a diagnostic publication latch from the cut through completion of the inline service call, including any immediate ordered application. It is not saved or hashed and changes no authoritative world state. The lane never accesses it: the sim checks it after the body, before ordered application, to reject a custom provider that bypassed its shell. Such a publication cannot be rolled back or retried; ordinary fixture/provider actions must use NativeActions. The lane's transactional output latch remains on the shell.

The sim first applies due output, then takes the next window's cut. Its one lane-originated world write at that cut reserves the existing fixed entity and invasion identity blocks, before the body starts. This sim-side reservation must stay at the cut: delaying it until adoption would change identities allocated by ordinary world services in between, including on the production schedule. The sim refreshes a private lane shell from the completed world and freezes conflict predicates. The body reads the window input and admits actions through the shell's ordinary native rules, with the shell's own barrier, identity reservation and output latch. Its deliveries reach AI-owned sinks; value-owned slices carry effects to the ordered world readmit at H. Speculative barrier reads use the shell's current player view, while the input oracle checks immutable cut values against the live world. The shell is rebuilt from a cut after load and contributes no saved bytes.

Inbox control switches can abandon plans and emit native cleanup. Those requests are staged on the shell as an owned prefix before the recoverable body attempt. Every attempt reconstructs its overlay with that prefix and prepends its records to the first output slice; a failed body attempt cannot drop an authoritative control switch's cleanup. In particular an invasion still waiting in an older slice is visible to control-switch cleanup before it is readmitted by the world.

The synchronized-day observer compares canonical world bytes after the sim-cut reservation and before body commit, and gates runtime/NativeActions live reads and writes at zero. This checks lane-originated mutations; the world's ordinary tick services continue between cuts and adoption. Every-tick saves, including finished output held for a full window, resume equally on 1/2/4/8 workers. A scripted fork at a stock window boundary saves every tick across real child entry and resumes through its return, leaving the production comparison arm unchanged. The production exactness receipt compares stock and synchronous-reference day output with BASE. Adopt1 also compares its full-game hash and delivery tallies with stock; the full-window delay compares its galaxy hash and the same tallies because retained finished output is included in the full-game hash. Both delayed schedules retain full-game peer and continuation equality.

## Interface

Inputs are the selected mode manager, synchronization frame, mode-frame time,
processing gate, six retained service deadlines, planet/world perception,
credits and fiscal notification, category/goal reservations, Lua plans,
freestore actors and outstanding build/movement blocks. Outputs include goal
activation, script writes, build requests and scheduled gameplay events.

Distinguish synchronization frames, retained galactic mode frames, tactical
child frames and Lua seconds. At ordinary 30 Hz the 0.1-second AI services
run every three synchronization frames. A child battle selects its own AI;
querying retained galactic perception does not advance galactic services.

planet, not an arbitrary tactical grid cell or every owned planet. Self selects

## Decisions and command admission

The inspected native routes distinguish immediate admission from deferred
locomotion, production progress and conflict arbitration. A blanket rule that
every AI action is queued for the next synchronized command pass is false for
the sourced direct movement and invasion interfaces. Equally, a returned block
does not prove travel or a tactical load has completed.

Already due synchronized player events execute before objects and outer AI
under GFS-01. Native AI decisions therefore read state left by those earlier
commands and object/fiscal commits. Direct AI effects may be read by later
plans, execution, players or global scripts in the same outer frame. GFS-A17
establishes retained queue traversal and tail insertion. Incoming
acceptance/sequence ties, the complete paused standalone mutation whitelist and
callbacks reentering other services remain UFO-01 / GAI-U03; no total order is
claimed for them.

In the remake, `ScriptedGalacticSession::step` submits current inputs, routes
previously retained script outputs, steps the world, services admitted AI then
story, and retains newly generated outputs sorted by issuer/sequence. Routed
outputs require an AI/script seat and enter the world's canonical
tick/seat/sequence ordering on the next world step. A shared routing cursor
prevents treating same-seat input and script orders as unrelated sequences.
This is the verified generic bridge policy, not evidence for native FoC action
latency; compare each actual writer and first consumer separately.
