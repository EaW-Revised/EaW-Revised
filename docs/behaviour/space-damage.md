# Space projectiles, damage types, shields and regeneration

## Applicability

- Product: Star Wars Empire at War: Forces of Corruption, tactical space, the M2 fixture
  ([m2-skirmish.md](../../plan/phase-2/m2-skirmish.md)). Data comes from the FoC profile of the
  retail corpus: `gameconstants.xml` (the damage constants and the `Damage_To_Armor_Mod` table),
  `projectiles.xml`, `hardpoints.xml` and the unit files (hashes under Sources in the M2 lock).
- Bounded question: what happens to a shot after it is fired ([space weapon fire](space-weapon-fire.md)
  W-06): how the projectile flies, what it hits or misses, how damage types meet armor types, how a
  shield absorbs damage and recharges, how the rest reaches the hull and the hardpoints of
  [space hardpoints](space-hardpoints.md), and what the energy pool pays for. P2-11
  (EAWR-74); the energy pool, the
  recharge phase and the verified defaults are EAWR-361.
- Source tags: **research** (the FoC debug build, read under the clean-room rule; evidence IDs
  PD-nn, CF-nn and IR-nn (EAWR-561) are opaque and their map stays private), **recording** (the P2-06 traces S-15, S-19 and S-28, fog
  revealed, [tests/fidelity](../../tests/fidelity/README.md)), **data** (a tag or value in the FoC
  files), **project** (a remake decision) and **unverified** (not established; the remake picks the
  least visible behaviour).
- Out of scope: visual projectile effects and sounds (EAWR-80), networking, squadron launch (EAWR-75),
  the AI (EAWR-79). The abilities' multipliers on weapons, shields and energy are in
  [space abilities](space-abilities.md) (EAWR-76).

## Interface

- Content: the durability table's damage rules (`DurabilityTable::damage`, built by
  `units::durability_table`) and per type its shield, refresh and armor types; the combat table's
  shot profile per weapon (projectile damage, damage type, speed, travel, inaccuracy) and collision
  box per type (`units::combat_table`). A session whose durability table has no damage rules
  behaves exactly as before EAWR-74: shots are events only and scripted damage is raw.
- State, hashed only with damage rules: per durable unit its shield, the frame its shield was last
  depleted, the frame of its last projectile hit, its energy and the frames of its next shield and
  energy recharges; the projectiles in flight and the next projectile ID
  ([replay format](../replay-format.md)).
- Outputs: the combat event `projectile_hit` (shooter, weapon, the unit reached, the hardpoint
  damaged, the projectile's position at the start of the frame and the contact point), the
  `hardpoint_destroyed` and `unit_destroyed` events of [space hardpoints](space-hardpoints.md), and
  each shielded instance's shield and maximum in the snapshot.
- Cadence: one projectile step per logical frame (1/30 s); per unit, one shield recharge every
  `ShieldRechargeIntervalInSecs` and one energy recharge every `EnergyRechargeIntervalInSecs`,
  each on the unit's own phase.

## Rules

### What a hit does

| Rule | Behaviour | Source |
|---|---|---|
| DG-01 | A projectile hit runs, in order: the diminishing-firepower factor (DG-05) and the combat modifiers (DG-26), the shield (DG-06 to DG-09), the hull armor (DG-10), then the hardpoint or hull (DG-11). | research PD-01, PD-14, CF-05 |
| DG-02 | Retail also scales damage by ability damage modes, base-shield vulnerability, the under-construction multiplier, a pending victory, a retreat, the other combat modifiers (the shooter's damage bonus and the target's defense bonuses from abilities, DG-26 names the one M2 reaches) and, for a shooter owned by an AI player, the AI difficulty's `Damage_Multiplier`. M2 uses none of them: the M2 fleet's abilities have no damage modes or combat modifiers ([space abilities](space-abilities.md) AB-02, AB-25), no bases or construction, the AI at Normal whose multiplier is 1.0. They are not modelled. | research PD-01, PD-17, CF-05; data |
| DG-26 | **A craft out of combat takes more damage** (EAWR-409). A projectile hit on a squadron craft is multiplied by 1 minus its type's `Out_Of_Combat_Defense_Adjustment` while the craft is out of combat, and by 1 otherwise. FoC authors -1.0 on every fighter and bomber that sets the tag, so such a hit does twice its damage. The craft's locomotor sets the adjustment each frame it flies: out of combat while its squadron has no target (FM-20, FM-21) and while the squadron flies its approach in formation beyond the strafe reach (FA-07); on a straight dive at the target beyond the reach after the approach (FA-01) and inside that reach (FA-02 to FA-06) it is 0. A hit reads the value its target's locomotor set this frame. FoC's combat modifier is (1 plus the shooter's damage bonuses) times (1 minus the target's defense bonuses); this adjustment is the only term M2 reaches (DG-02). It applies to every projectile hit independently of DG-05's two gates below (confirmed in the decompile: the out-of-combat multiplier sits after the diminishing-firepower block but outside the two checks that gate it). FoC's directed combat clears the adjustment each frame and only the path approach sets it again (research FS-01, FS-02, EAWR-469), so a squadron is out of combat beyond the strafe reach only while it still follows the path it planned toward the target; the remake models that approach as FA-07, whose path shape is unverified (space-fighters G-F8). Recording: in S-28 every hit on a craft 600 to 710 units from the corvette takes 22.4 (two times 8 x 1.4) or about 14 (two times the DG-05-reduced 7), and every hit within 480 units 11.2 or about 7, on all eighteen craft of runs 2 and 3; the strafe reach is 500 plus the corvette's radius. The remake caps the product at the curve's bound (a Q24 limit no hit reaches). | research CF-04, CF-05; data; recording S-28 |
| DG-05 | A projectile hit is multiplied by the `Diminishing_Firepower` curve only when both gate: the shooter's allow-diminishing-firepower mode flag is on (default on; FoC's data turns it off only for a shooter under a MAXIMUM_FIREPOWER-style ability, herocompanies.xml's Veers AT-AT and a unique unit in uniqueunits.xml, neither in the M2 fleet), and the projectile's internal damage type (`Internal_Damage_Type`, a hard-coded system separate from the XML armor damage types per gameconstants.xml's own comment) is the misc type, the default (verified in the type constructor; only Force Whirlwind and Remote Bomb, land abilities, author another one). When both hold, the curve is evaluated at the seconds since the target's previous diminishing-eligible hit (frames / 30; a unit never hit that way before counts 999,999 frames): the natural cubic spline through the constant's (x, y) pairs, with the end values outside them; FoC's `0, 0.6, 0.3, 0.7, 0.9, 0.9, 1, 1, 2, 1` gives 0.6 for a hit in the same frame, 0.69 eight frames later, 1.0 at one second, up to about 1.18 at one and a half, and 1.0 from two seconds. Two eligible hits in one frame: the second gets 0.6. The remake caps the factor at 256 (the multiplier bound, project; FoC's curve never comes near it). When either gate fails the hit is unscaled and the target's last-hit frame does not move, so a later eligible hit is unaffected by the ones the gates skipped; DG-26 still applies on a gate-skipped hit. Every M2 shooter and projectile has both gates on, so M2 behaviour is unchanged from before EAWR-440. | research PD-07; recording S-15 (every first-burst hit size matches) |
| DG-06 | A unit has a shield only when its type has the `SHIELDED` behaviour; its maximum is `Shield_Points` (not scaled by the space health multiplier). A hit that still has damage meets the shield first: the damage times the `Damage_To_Armor_Mod` of the projectile's damage type against the type's `Shield_Armor_Type`. The shield absorbs as much as it has. A projectile without `Projectile_Does_Shield_Damage` passes the shield untouched. | research PD-02, PD-03; data; recording S-15 (800 shield, first hit exactly 15) |
| DG-07 | What the shield does not absorb is divided by that same multiplier again before it goes on; with a zero multiplier the full damage goes on. | research PD-02 |
| DG-08 | A shield set to zero starts the depletion effect unless it already runs; the effect lasts while fewer than `Depleted_Shield_Disable_Time` seconds (5.0: 150 frames) have passed since. A shield set above zero outside the effect ends it. A shield set to zero also ends an active `DEFEND` ([space abilities](space-abilities.md) AB-17). | research PD-09; recording S-15 (0 at tick 698) |
| DG-09 | During the effect the shield absorbs nothing; each hit with damage adds `Depleted_Shield_Damage_Increment` seconds (0.0 in FoC) to it, up to the current frame. | research PD-03 |
| DG-10 | The rest is multiplied by the `Damage_To_Armor_Mod` of the damage type against the type's `Armor_Type`. A projectile without `Projectile_Does_Hitpoint_Damage` stops here. | research PD-05 |
| DG-11 | The rest damages a hardpoint or the hull. A shot aimed at a hardpoint of the unit it reached goes by its aim (DG-39). Otherwise, with collision meshes (DG-36), the projectile's collided mesh decides: a mesh whose name is some hardpoint's `Collision_Mesh` (ASCII case ignored, the first such hardpoint in `HardPoints` order) sends it to that hardpoint when it is destroyable, and while it has health it alone takes the damage (HD-02, HD-03, HD-21); any other mesh, the shield mesh included, sends it to the hull (HD-20). A hardpoint's mesh stops colliding once the hardpoint is destroyed (DG-38), so a destroyed hardpoint never takes a hit. Without meshes (a table that has only the box) the hardpoint the shot aimed at takes it when the projectile reached the unit it was fired at, as before EAWR-536. | research PD-06 (the collided mesh names the hardpoint; the debug build looks it up by `Collision_Mesh`), recording S-45 and S-49 (EAWR-536 hit log: hits on `HP_*_Coll` meshes, none after that hardpoint's death) |
| DG-39 | **The aim decides, not the mesh** (EAWR-669). A projectile that reaches the unit it was fired at, from a shot aimed at one of that unit's hardpoints (every shot at a unit with a live targetable hardpoint, space-weapon-fire W-10), is routed by that hardpoint's `Collision_Mesh`, whichever mesh it met (the hull, `COLLISION`, `shield` or another hardpoint's): the first hardpoint in `HardPoints` order whose `Collision_Mesh` equals it (ASCII case ignored) takes the damage when it is destroyable and has health. If that hardpoint is already destroyed the rest is lost: neither it nor the hull takes anything. A non-destroyable one, or an aimed hardpoint without `Collision_Mesh`, sends it to the hull. The shield still absorbs first (DG-06). The mesh met still decides whether and where the shot hits (DG-36 to DG-38). So a capital ship's hull loses health only through the hardpoint coupling (space-hardpoints HS-02) while shots aim at its hardpoints; it dies with its last destroyable hardpoint (HD-21), not through its hull. Shots aimed at no hardpoint (a unit without live targetable ones), and hits on a unit other than the one fired at, keep DG-11. | research PD-34 (the damage entry replaces the collided mesh name with the aimed hardpoint's `Collision_Mesh` when the projectile's original target is the damaged unit and it names a hardpoint; the projectile hit path does not opt out), PD-06; recording S-45 and S-49 (EAWR-536, both seeds): every sampled drop of the Nebulon-B's hull sits on the HS-02 cap min(1, S/T + 0.2); direct hull loss 0 over 1522 to 2326 ticks, with the shield down from tick 1032 and hundreds of laser hits on its hull meshes |
| DG-12 | The damage type of a hardpoint's shot is the hardpoint's `Damage_Type`, else the projectile's, else `Damage_Default`. A pair that the table does not author multiplies by 1: FoC fills the whole damage-by-armor table with 1 before it reads the `Damage_To_Armor_Mod` rows, and reads a negative entry as 1 as well. A pair authored twice keeps the later row. | research PD-10, PD-16, PD-27; data |

### Shields over time

| Rule | Behaviour | Source |
|---|---|---|
| DG-13 | Every `ShieldRechargeIntervalInSecs` × 30 + 0.5 frames, truncated (3.0 s: 90 frames), a shield gains `Shield_Refresh_Rate`, up to its maximum. Each unit keeps its own phase: its first recharge comes a synchronized draw of 0 to interval − 1 frames after it enters, then one every interval. | research PD-08, PD-26; recording S-15 (the Tartan gains 10 at ticks 92, 182, 272 and so on, the Nebulon-B at 97, 187 and so on) |
| DG-14 | On a type with shield-generator hardpoints the gain is scaled by the share of them not destroyed. | research PD-08 |
| DG-15 | During the depletion effect a recharge stops at `Depleted_Shield_Regen_Cap` (0.25); after it the recharge clears the effect. | research PD-08, PD-09; recording S-15 (0.25 at tick 722, kept while hit) |
| DG-16 | A unit with an energy pool pays for each recharge from it, and gains nothing when the pool is short (EN-04). | research PD-04, PD-08, PD-23 |
| DG-17 | When the last shield generator of a type that has them is destroyed, the shield drops to zero and never recharges again (HD-12). | research PD-19 |

### Energy pool

| Rule | Behaviour | Source |
|---|---|---|
| EN-01 | A type with the `POWERED` behaviour has an energy pool whose maximum is `Energy_Capacity`. A unit enters with a full pool. A type without `POWERED` has no pool. In M2 every ship, craft and station is `POWERED`: the Tartan 4,500, the Nebulon-B and the Acclamator 3,000, the Corellian corvette 1,500, the fighters and bombers 500 and the stations 5,000. | research PD-22; data |
| EN-02 | Every `EnergyRechargeIntervalInSecs` × 30 + 0.5 frames, truncated (5.0 s: 150 frames), the pool gains `Energy_Refresh_Rate`, up to its maximum, on the unit's own phase as in DG-13. A type that needs base power loses all its energy while its base is unpowered; no M2 type does. | research PD-22, PD-26; data |
| EN-03 | *Project:* in one frame the object weapon draws first (targeting), then the pool recharges, then the shield. FoC services a unit's behaviours in the order it keeps them in; that order is not traced. | project |
| EN-04 | A shield recharge (DG-13 to DG-15) that raises the shield costs the rise times `EnergyToShieldExchangeRate` (5.0). The cost follows the rise as the maximum and the depletion cap leave it, so a nearly full shield costs little. A pool holding at least the cost pays it and the shield rises; a pool holding less pays nothing and the shield keeps its value until the next recharge. A type without a pool recharges free. | research PD-23 |
| EN-05 | The unit's own weapon (`Projectile_Types`) draws `Projectile_Energy_Per_Shot` from the pool for each shot. When its countdown allows a shot, a pool holding less than the cost fires nothing: the countdown stays at zero and the burst keeps its shot (the first shot of a burst; a later pulse is spent first, W-06a), so it fires on the first frame the pool holds enough. The cost is paid after the shot is fired. A unit without a pool never fires a weapon that costs energy. | research PD-24 |
| EN-06 | Hardpoint weapons never draw energy, whatever their projectile's `Projectile_Energy_Per_Shot`: the FoC capital lasers author 10 to 20 a shot, and it never applies to them. The energy check and drain sit only in the unit's own weapon service. That service fires the unit type's `Projectile_Types` entry from the unit's firing bones; the hardpoint index it carries (or its "none" value) is the target's aimed hardpoint, used to pick an ability's projectile override, not the shooter's weapon. A hardpoint fires through its own routine, which creates its projectile directly and looks up no energy behaviour. Nothing else calls the check or the drain. (Re-verified for the EAWR-470 review.) | research PD-24, PD-25 |
| EN-07 | **Ion drain** (EAWR-561). A projectile with `Projectile_Does_Energy_Damage` drains the pool of the unit it hits. The drain comes after the shield (DG-06) and before the division of DG-07: what the shield left, still multiplied by the damage type's `Damage_To_Armor_Mod` against the unit's `Shield_Armor_Type`, is taken from the pool up to what the pool holds, and only the rest is divided back and goes on. A unit with a pool is drained even without a shield, at the multiplier of its shield armor type; a unit without a pool loses nothing. During the shield's depletion effect (DG-09) the whole scaled hit drains the pool. Only a projectile drains: scripted damage (DG-20) never does. A drained pool then pays for no shield recharge (EN-04), so an ion weapon keeps a shield down. | research IR-01, IR-02; data |
| EN-08 | A projectile with `Projectile_Disables_Engines_When_Power_Drained` that drains anything also takes the unit's engines offline for its `Projectile_Disable_Engines_Duration`. The tag defaults to off, and no M2 projectile sets it (FoC's small ion cannon, on the B-wing and TIE Defender, does). Not modelled. | research IR-01, IR-03; data |

In M2 only the object weapons of the X-wing, TIE Fighter and TIE Interceptor cost energy (5 a
shot); at their burst rates they spend well under the 300 a pool regains every 150 frames. The
shields decide who can run dry. A recharge costs 50 on the Tartan, 75 on the corvette and 250
on the Nebulon-B, all within what their pools regain. The Acclamator's costs 310 every 90 frames
against 400 every 150. After about two minutes of recharging without a break its pool runs dry,
and it then gains about 80 shield every 150 frames instead of about 103.

### Ion weapons

The M2 ion weapons (EAWR-561). An ion shot is the rock-paper-scissors counter to shields: it takes
the shield down and drains the pool, but never damages the hull.

| Weapon | Projectile | Damage | Against shields | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The MC80's two front ion cannons (`HP_Calamari_Cruiser_Weapon_FL`, `_FR`, EAWR-537) | `Proj_Ship_Ion_Cannon_Large` | 40 (the hardpoint's own, over the projectile's 20, DG-25), `Damage_IonCannon_LRG` | x3 capital and frigate shields, x2 corvette and fighter shields | shield damage and drain (EN-07), no hull damage, no stun |
| The Y-wing squadron's `ION_CANNON_SHOT` ([space abilities](space-abilities.md) AB-60 to AB-69) | `Proj_Ion_Cannon_Medium_Laser_Blue` | 50, `Damage_Fighter` (the firing hardpoint's type first, DG-12) | as the damage type authors | shield damage and a stun (IS-01 to IS-07); no drain, no hull damage |

No level-1 station, corvette, frigate or fighter of the M2 roster carries an ion weapon; FoC's
higher-level stations and several heroes and capitals outside M2 do. A laser or turbolaser hit
damages the shield and then the hull (DG-06 to DG-11) and never drains; an ion hit damages the
shield and drains, and its hull share is cut to nothing at DG-10 because it has no
`Projectile_Does_Hitpoint_Damage`.

| Rule | Behaviour | Source |
|---|---|---|
| IS-01 | A projectile with `Projectile_Ion_Stun_On_Detonation` stuns the unit it hits, after its damage (DG-01) and whether or not the hit did damage. It carries its stun: `Projectile_Ion_Stun_Duration` seconds as trunc(seconds x 30) frames (at least one), `Projectile_Ion_Stun_Speed_Reduction_Percent` and `Projectile_Ion_Stun_Shot_Rate_Reduction_Percent` (fractions: FoC's 0.5 halves), and `Projectile_Ion_Stun_Stack_Duration`. FoC's Y-wing shot: 1.0 s (30 frames), 0.5 and 0.5, stacking. A hit that destroys the unit stuns nothing. | research IR-04, IR-05; data |
| IS-02 | Only a unit whose type has the `ION_STUN_EFFECT` behaviour is stunned. In M2 that is every capital ship, frigate, corvette and station; fighters and bombers are never stunned. FoC also skips a unit that is in hyperspace; no unit in an M2 battle is, so the remake stuns every unit with the behaviour. | research IR-04, IR-05, IR-14; data |
| IS-03 | A unit is stunned from the hit's frame until its end frame; at the end frame the stun ends (its effect hides and its cuts stop). | research IR-04, IR-06 |
| IS-04 | A stacking shot on a unit whose stun ends at or after the hit's frame adds its frames to that end; any other hit ends the stun its frames after the hit's frame, replacing a longer one. Three Y-wing hits in one second keep a unit stunned for three. | research IR-04 |
| IS-05 | While stunned, the unit's maximum speed is multiplied by 1 minus the speed reduction, after the ability speed multiplier and the lost-engines factor (HD-11, AB-24). FoC changes only the maximum; acceleration and deceleration keep their values (FoC's own reading of them was not traced: **unverified**). The cut reaches a move when it is planned: a move under way keeps its speed when a stun starts or ends, and a move ordered while a unit is stunned keeps the cut speed for its whole length. In a retail recording, a Tartan cruising under a move order kept its speed exactly through about three seconds of stacked stuns (G-D9). FoC reads the cut maximum through the type's speed (IR-07), and the locomotor follows a path it has already made (IR-14). Whether a move planned during a stun speeds up when the stun ends is not traced (**unverified**). The latest shot's reductions apply: FoC keeps one cut per projectile type and, across types, the smallest cut; M2 has one stun type. | research IR-04, IR-07, IR-08 |
| IS-06 | While stunned, each hardpoint weapon fires at a rate r = 1 minus the shot rate reduction. After a burst its recharge (W-rules with AB-21's weapon delay) is divided by r when r is in (0, 1), rounded half up, and its next burst has trunc(pulse count x r + 0.5) shots (at least one fires: an empty count recharges after each shot); between two shots of a burst the gap (with AB-21's weapon delay) is divided by r, rounded half up, and a rate of zero ends the burst. A countdown already running keeps its length. FoC's 0.5 doubles the capital lasers' recharge and gaps and cuts a burst of five to three. The unit's own object weapon is not slowed (**unverified**; no stunnable M2 type has one). | research IR-09, IR-08 |
| IS-07 | A stun ends an active `DEFEND` (early, AB-12), and `DEFEND` may not switch on while the unit is stunned ([space abilities](space-abilities.md) AB-14). | research IR-04; AB-R02 |
| IS-08 | A projectile with a `Projectile_Ion_Stun_Radius` above zero stuns every enemy unit within the radius instead of the unit it hit. No M2 projectile authors a radius; the loader refuses one (not modelled). The Y-wing shot's other stun tags (`Projectile_Stun_On_Detonation` and its radius of 300) reach only units with the `STUNNABLE` behaviour, which no M2 space type has, and clear stealth, which no M2 unit uses: not modelled. | research IR-05, IR-10; data |
| IS-09 | A stunned unit shows its model's ion-stun emitters (proxy prefix `pi`) until the stun ends, by the code flag of [battle presentation](battle-presentation.md) BP-43: an emitter the model authors hidden stays hidden. The M2 models' only such proxy, the Tartan's `pi_damage_elec_cap00`, is authored hidden, so in M2 a stun shows no effect of its own (the ion bolt's hit and the shield ripple still show). A retail recording (EAWR-561, G-D9) shows the stunned Tartan covered in a blue electric effect for about three seconds; its source is not identified (**unverified**: the ion bolts' impact on the shield, not the hidden proxy), and the eye check compares it with the remake. The snapshot carries each stunned instance's frames left. | research IR-04, IR-06; BP-43 |

### Projectiles

| Rule | Behaviour | Source |
|---|---|---|
| DG-21 | A shot (W-06) of a weapon with a projectile becomes a projectile at the shot's origin. It moves from the next frame on. | research PD-12 |
| DG-22 | It flies in a straight line toward the shot's aim point at the projectile's `Max_Speed` per frame (25 for the M2 capital lasers). A `MISSILE`-category projectile homes instead (MS-01 to MS-07). Rockets, bombs and grenades have paths of their own in FoC; no M2 weapon fires one. | research PD-12, PD-30; recording S-15 (a shot at tick 4 hits at 35, about 790 units away) |
| DG-23 | Its travel ends at the hardpoint's `Fire_Range_Distance` (an object weapon: the projectile's `Projectile_Max_Flight_Distance`) plus the height between its origin and aim point. Retail adds the target's soft radius, which is not loaded (zero, P-04). | research PD-10, PD-18 |
| DG-24 | A hardpoint's shot is aimed off its led point (space-weapon-fire W-10, W-11) by a draw in [-r, r] on each axis, r = the planar distance from the weapon midpoint to the aim point before the lead × the inaccuracy / `Fire_Range_Distance`. The inaccuracy is the `Fire_Inaccuracy_Distance` of the first row that names one of the target's categories, **doubled**: the debug build returns the authored distance plus the distance times a mode factor that is 1 outside land battles (on land it is one minus the weather's accuracy modifier), so in space every shot scatters twice as far as the XML says (EAWR-536). A type's `Targeting_Fire_Inaccuracy_Fixed_Radius` replaces the whole rule with a fixed radius; no M2 type authors one. **The object weapon's scatter** (EAWR-607) is the same draw, with r = the spatial distance from the firing bone to the led point × the type's `Targeting_Fire_Inaccuracy` row for the target, doubled in space as above / its `Targeting_Max_Attack_Distance` (as authored, without modifiers); its cone (space-weapon-fire W-09) is then tested on the scattered point. No space unit in FoC's data authors `Targeting_Fire_Inaccuracy` (ground units do), so no M2 object weapon scatters. The `Fire_Inaccuracy_Distance` rows every FoC fighter authors are not read for its object weapon: the debug build parses that tag only into hardpoint data. The S-97 and S-98 shot logs agree: retail's fighters land 87 to 93 % of their shots on other fighters. | research PD-11, CF-03 (EAWR-536: the doubling); debug build (EAWR-607: the object weapon's scatter, its getter, and the tag table rows of both tags); data; recording S-97, S-98 (EAWR-607 shot log) |
| DG-25 | Damage: the hardpoint's own `Projectile_Damage` when it authors a positive one, else the projectile's `Projectile_Damage` (EAWR-561). Of the space hardpoints only the MC80's (`HP_Calamari_Cruiser_Weapon_*`: 40 on its two forward ion cannons, whose projectile authors 20, and 20 on the other four), the Victory's and the Assault Frigate's author one. | research PD-10, IR-12; data |
| DG-30 | Each frame, after targeting and before the frame's commands, every projectile in flight at the start of the frame flies one step. It hits the first unit, along the step, of a team other than its owner's that the step meets (DG-36, DG-37; without meshes, whose collision box the step enters); a tie goes to the lower ID. On a hit it is spent. FoC takes the first unit its collision tree hands out that reports a hit, not the nearest (*project:* the remake keeps the nearest). | research PD-12, PD-13; EAWR-536 |
| DG-31 | A type's collision box is the model-space box around its model's collidable meshes, each placed by its bone's bind frame, scaled by `Scale_Factor` and turned by FoC's +90 degree model turn ([R-ROT-01](p1-effective-environment.md)). A type without collidable meshes cannot be hit. Since EAWR-536 the box and the box around every mesh only gate the mesh test of DG-36; a table without meshes (the synthetic contract fixtures) is hit by the box itself. | project |
| DG-32 | Any unit of another team in the path is hit, not only the target; friendly units and units of the owner's team are passed. | research PD-13 |
| DG-33 | A projectile that hits nothing adds its speed to its travel and ends when the travel reaches its maximum: an out-of-range miss. The whole step is tested for a hit before the travel is counted, so the step that ends the flight can still hit up to one step (`Max_Speed`) past the maximum; the step is not clipped. The flight ends by distance, not by `Projectile_Max_Lifetime` (retail uses the lifetime only for a zero maximum travel or a grenade). | research PD-12, PD-21 |
| DG-34 | Hits apply in ascending projectile ID. A unit killed by an earlier hit leaves at once; a later projectile that reached it is spent without effect. | project |
| DG-35 | `Allow_Opportunity_Fire_When_Idle` and `Allow_Opportunity_Fire_When_Targeting` default to yes (W-04): FoC's hardpoint data sets both unless the hardpoint says otherwise. | research PD-20; recording S-15 (the untagged Nebulon-B laser fires at the Tartan) |
| DG-36 | **Meshes, not a box** (EAWR-536). A projectile's step hits a unit when it meets a triangle of one of the unit's collision meshes: every mesh of its model with collision enabled, hidden or not (`COLLISION`, the hull and `Lighting` meshes, the `HP_*_blast` meshes and `shield` on the Nebulon-B), then every collision-enabled mesh of each hardpoint's `Model_To_Attach`, placed at the hardpoint's `Attachment_Bone` (where the `HP_*_Coll` meshes that `Collision_Mesh` names live). The first triangle along the step wins, both faces count; its mesh decides where the damage goes (DG-11). *Project:* the test is exact on a grid of 1/32 unit in the unit's frame, with a tree of boxes per mesh; mesh vertices must lie within 4096 units of the unit and a shot may move at most 4096 units a frame; on a tie the earlier mesh wins. | research PD-06 (the collided sub-object's name reaches the damage), CF-06 (the segment is tested against each renderable mesh with collision enabled, gated by its box); recording S-43 to S-45 (EAWR-536 hit log: hits on `SHADOW`, `shield`, `COLLISION`, `RV_NebulonB`, `HP_*_blast`, `HP_*_Coll`) |
| DG-37 | **The craft sphere.** A type whose `Collision_Box_Modifier` exceeds 1 (every M2 fighter and bomber: 2.0) is also hit, when its meshes are not, by a step whose start, end or midpoint lies strictly closer to the unit's position than the modifier times the largest half extent of its **world box**, but only when the step meets that world box: the collision box (DG-31) turned by the unit and made axis-aligned again, so it grows as the craft turns (a half extent is the sum over the model axes of the rotation's magnitude times that axis's half extent). Such a hit lands at the step's start and on the hull. The debug build tests a unit for a projectile only when the step meets the unit's box in its collision tree, and that box is the object's world bounds: its model's collision bounds turned into the world (EAWR-607); before, the remake tested the sphere wherever the step came near, which made every fighter a target of twice its box's size on all sides. *Project:* the world box is taken from the unit's pose of the frame; when FoC's tree refreshes its box is not traced (**unverified**). | research CF-06; debug build (EAWR-607: the tree's box test, the world bounds); data |
| DG-38 | **Which meshes collide.** The mesh named `shield` (ASCII case ignored) collides only with a projectile that does shield damage (`Projectile_Does_Shield_Damage`), and only while the unit's shield is above zero: the lasers of S-45 hit it 219 times with the Nebulon-B's shield up and once after it fell, the torpedoes and missiles never. The meshes of a hardpoint's attached model stop colliding once that hardpoint is destroyed: in S-45 and S-49 no hit met a destroyed hardpoint's `HP_*_Coll` after its death frame. Identifying the shield mesh by name is **unverified** (the debug build keeps a shield sub-object index whose source was not traced); every M2 model with a shield mesh names it `shield`. | recording S-45, S-49 (EAWR-536 hit log); research CF-06 (a collision mask bit that only shield-damaging projectiles set) |

### Missiles

| Rule | Behaviour | Source |
|---|---|---|
| MS-01 | A projectile whose `Projectile_Category` is `MISSILE` homes on the unit it was fired at. | MS-02 | At launch a missile faces the shot's aim point after scatter (DG-24) and holds the unit it was fired at and the hardpoint it aimed at. A facing is a yaw from the planar direction and a pitch from the height over the planar distance. | research PD-32 |
| MS-03 | Each frame a missile that holds its target steers toward its aimed hardpoint's current position, or toward the target's position when it aimed at no hardpoint. | MS-04 | It turns toward that point the short way, by at most its turn rate in yaw and, separately, in pitch, then moves `Max_Speed` along its new facing. Its speed stays `Max_Speed`: FoC's data sets no projectile acceleration. | research PD-31 |
| MS-05 | When its target leaves the session (it dies or is removed), a missile drops the target and flies on along its last facing. It never takes another target. | MS-06 | Hits, travel and expiry are those of every projectile (DG-23, DG-30 to DG-34): it hits the first hostile unit in its path, and it counts its speed as travel each frame, so a missile that has to turn runs out of travel sooner. | research PD-12, PD-31 |
| MS-07 | *Project:* the facing is computed in Q24 (DP-02); the target's position stands for FoC's height-adjusted target position, which is not traced; a missile keeps steering at a hardpoint that has been destroyed while its unit lives, as FoC keeps the hardpoint. | project |

### Project choices

| Rule | Choice |
|---|---|
| DP-01 | The flight is one partitioned phase, `projectiles` ([phase map](../simulation.md#phase-map)); each projectile reads the targeting phase's immutable world view and writes only its own slot. The serial commit applies the hits in ascending projectile ID, then this frame's shots join as new projectiles in event order. |
| DP-02 | Arithmetic is Q24 with one rounding per operation ([fixed-point](../fixed-point.md)); retail computes in binary32. The spline is evaluated in Q24 too, within 2^-20 of retail's values. Retail's float rounding also leaks about 6e-5 of each absorbed hit to the hull (S-15: 749.99994 after the first hits); the remake does not. |
| DP-03 | Model points of the combat table (hardpoints, fire bones, target bones, the collision box) take FoC's +90 degree model turn, as the viewer does; before EAWR-74 the table used raw model space. |
| DP-04 | A decimal time converted to whole frames (recharge, pulse delay) no longer loses a frame to Q24 rounding: 0.2 s × 30 is 6 frames, as retail's binary32 gives and S-15's shots are spaced. |
| DP-06 | The ion stun (EAWR-561) applies in the serial hit commit, in ascending projectile ID like every hit (DP-01); the stun's end and the replan it causes are serviced in the partitioned `abilities` phase of the tick, each unit in its own slot. The stun's cuts are Q24 fractions; the stunned recharge and pulse counts are rounded exactly as retail's binary32 `+ 0.5` truncation for every M2 value. |
| DP-05 | DG-05's two gates are read once, at the shot: the shooter's flag from its durability profile and the projectile's internal-damage-type flag from its shot profile, both carried on the in-flight projectile to the hit that resolves it. Retail re-reads the shooter's live flag at the hit; no M2 ability changes the flag ([space abilities](space-abilities.md) AB-25), so no M2 shooter's flag ever changes between its shot and its hit, and the two are equivalent for M2. |
