# Space fog of war in the world

## Applicability

- Product: Star Wars Empire at War: Forces of Corruption, tactical space, the live M2 battle
  ([m2-skirmish.md](../../plan/phase-2/m2-skirmish.md)). Owner request (world fog rendering): "if using fog-of-war
  we need to visualise this as well in-game".
- Bounded question: how FoC draws the local player's space fog of war in the 3D world, what it
  looks like, how fast it follows the fog, and how the remake draws it from the live session.
  Which units the player sees is [space-visibility.md](space-visibility.md) (V-01 to V-17); the
  minimap's fog layer is [foc-minimap.md](foc-minimap.md) MM-10.
- Source tags: **data** (a tag or texture in the FoC files), **research** (the FoC debug build;
  evidence IDs FWE-1 to FWE-11 map to notes kept outside the repository), **runtime** (retail
  stills taken on the rig with fog on and with `-NoFog`, FWE-12), **project** (a remake decision).
- Implemented by `presentation::space::FogField` (`include/eawr/presentation/space/fog_field.hpp`,
  the engine-free rules) and the viewer's `LiveFogView` (the plane). Presentation only: nothing is
  read back into the simulation and the headless hashes are unchanged.
- fog visibility fades: how a unit itself fades as the fog reveals or hides it (not the plane) is
  `presentation::space::UnitFade` (`include/eawr/presentation/space/unit_fade.hpp`), driven by
  `LiveSessionView::frame()` and applied through `GodotRenderer::set_unit_opacity`. Also
  presentation only.

## Interface

- Inputs: `gameconstants.xml` (FW-01), the grid tile texture (FW-03), and every rendered frame
  the local player's fog cells after the newest tick (which the live session publishes with each
  tick, `LiveSession::fog_at`) and the presented tick. A battle without fog rules gives the local
  team's revealers (position and reveal range) and the playable rectangle instead.
- Output: one textured plane in the world at `SpaceFOWHeight`, whose texture has one texel per fog
  cell.

## Rules

| Rule | Behaviour | Source |
|---|---|---|
| FW-01 | The space fog reads four `gameconstants.xml` values: `SpaceFOWColor` (255, 255, 255, 255), `SpaceFOWHeight` (−80), `DesiredSpaceFOWCellSize` (100) and `SpaceFOWRegrowTime` (6 s). The last entry of a tag wins; a missing or unreadable one keeps the FoC value and is reported. | data; research FWE-3, FWE-4 |
| FW-02 | The fog is one flat, horizontal plane at world height `SpaceFOWHeight`, drawn every frame with the transparent draws. It is depth tested but writes no depth, is seen from both sides, is unlit and is alpha blended over what is already drawn (source alpha, one minus source alpha). Units above the plane hide it; the backdrop behind it shows through. | research FWE-4, FWE-9 |
| FW-03 | The plane's look comes from the grid tile `W_Space_FOW_Grid` (128 × 128): a square of alpha 18 (of 255) with a 10-texel clear margin on every side, so the fog reads as faint tiles separated by clear lines. Only the tile's alpha is used; its colour is not. | data; research FWE-2, FWE-9, FWE-10 |
| FW-04 | The pixel's colour is the fog texture's colour and its alpha is the fog texture's alpha times the tile's alpha. The tile repeats 200 times across the fog rectangle on each axis, phased from the world origin. On Coruscant (13,000 units) a tile is 65 units, a little under a cell. A fully fogged pixel therefore adds about 18 of white inside a tile and nothing in the gaps, as measured on the retail stills (+18, +18, +17 on the sky). | research FWE-9; runtime FWE-12 |
| FW-05 | The fog texture follows the fog every rendered frame. Fades are counted in logical frames (30 a second): a paused battle holds them, fast forward runs them faster. A fading-out cell drops int(238 / (`SpaceFOWRegrowTime` × 30 / 16)) / 16 a frame on the fade scale of FW-09, which is 21/16 for 6 s. | research FWE-4, FWE-6 |
| FW-06 | Space fog has no explored state: a cell is either revealed (held by a revealer or, in FoC, still lingering) or fogged, however often it was seen before, and there is no black shroud. The plane is the only thing the fog draws: no space unit shader reads the fog texture (only land props and grass do), so a unit is never tinted by fog; an enemy under fog is hidden by visibility (V-16), not drawn darker. The plane draws no last-known image of anything. | research FWE-6, FWE-11 |
| FW-07 | The fog texture has one texel per fog cell of `DesiredSpaceFOWCellSize`, row 0 at the top (largest Y), over the rectangle FoC's fog grid covers: the map's bounds. The remake uses the session's fog grid (the map's declared extents, V-18; Coruscant 130 × 130 cells from (−6500, 6500)). A battle without fog rules uses the camera's target bounds widened to whole cells around their centre (the minimap's playable rectangle, MM-02). | research FWE-8; project |
| FW-08 | The plane shows the local player's own fog grid (V-11 to V-19): a cell at 255 is held (by a revealer's circle, or a fire flash, V-19), one from 1 to 238 lingers after its release, 0 is fogged. The remake draws the cells the live session holds after the newest tick, so the plane, the minimap's fog (MM-10) and which enemies are shown (V-16) agree. A battle without fog rules has no cells: there a cell is held while its centre lies within the planar reveal range (inclusive) of a local-team revealer in the newest snapshot, the minimap's test. | research FWE-4; project |
| FW-09 | Each cell has a fade value on FoC's scale. Its shown intensity (0 fogged, 255 clear) is int(min(v / 16, 1) × 255) below 239 and int((v − 237) / 18 × 255) from 239 to 255. A cell that becomes held while fully fogged starts at 239 and rises one a logical frame to 255, so it clears over 16 frames from intensity 28; one still fading out (0 < v < 16) restarts at 255 − v; one that still shows clear (v ≥ 16) goes straight to 255. | research FWE-5, FWE-6 |
| FW-10 | A released cell lingers and then fades out: its grid value drops by the service step (21 for 6 s) every 16 logical frames (V-12), and the plane spreads each drop over those frames, showing the grid value less the FW-05 step for each frame since the grid's last service. Values 238 down to 16 all show clear, so the cell stays clear for most of `SpaceFOWRegrowTime` and fades only at the end, about 13 frames from 16 to 0. A lingering cell whose fade-in is unfinished finishes it first. Without fog cells (FW-08) there is no linger: a released cell starts the fade at 16 at once, or from the matching point of an unfinished fade-in. | research FWE-5, FWE-6; project |
| FW-11 | Intensity i takes the colour `SpaceFOWColor` × (255 − i) / 255 on all four channels: fully fogged is `SpaceFOWColor`, clear is transparent black. | research FWE-3 |
| FW-12 | The texture's outermost ring of cells is always fogged. The other cells are blurred with the 3 × 3 kernel (1, 2, 1) × (1, 2, 1) / 16, rounded, over the unblurred intensities, the ring included, which softens the circles' edges by about a cell. FoC updates only the cells in view (plus two); the remake updates them all, which shows the same. | research FWE-7 |
| FW-13 | The plane covers the fog rectangle and nothing beyond it: past the map's edge space is drawn without fog, with a sharp edge. The fog texture is sampled with bilinear filtering, so the change from fogged to revealed is soft over about a cell. | runtime FWE-12; research FWE-9 (clamped texture) |
| FW-15 | The planet and the backdrop objects are drawn over the fog, as in the retail stills; the sky is fogged. The remake draws the plane after the sky's transparent layers and skips every pixel where an opaque surface already lies behind the plane, which leaves the planet and far backdrop objects unfogged and fogs the sky (which writes no depth). | runtime FWE-12; project (the mechanism) |
| FW-14 | A player whose map is revealed has no fogged cell, so the plane draws nothing. `--eawr-live-reveal on` (every unit type sees the whole map) therefore draws no plane at all. | research (reveal of the entire map on init, FWE-6); project |
| FW-16 | fog visibility fades: a unit fades in or out on its own, not on its cell's fade (FW-05, FW-09, FW-10). FoC gives every unit its own hide-when-fogged state whose opacity eases from 0 (hidden) to 1 (shown) or back on a critically damped, no-overshoot curve with a 0.25 s characteristic time; the cell's held/fogged test (V-16) only sets which end it eases towards. | research FWE-13 |
| FW-17 | The ease's target follows V-16 at each visibility recheck. The debug build uses a 30-logical-frame countdown for a settled unit; while the absolute difference between target and opacity is at least 0.0001 it rechecks every service and restarts that countdown. Thus a settled unit can react up to one second after a visibility change; the remake rechecks every tick, which can only show the same flip sooner, never a different one (G-FW8 covers the few fog states V-16 does not read at all). A unit already at its starting state when it first appears (the battle's opening roster, or one born since) shows there at once; only a later change eases. | research FWE-13; project (recheck cadence) |
| FW-18 | The unit's own model keeps drawing throughout the ease, at wherever it has truly moved to since (not a frozen last-seen pose): it is hidden only once the ease reaches 0.025 or below, and shown again as soon as it rises back above, continuing from wherever the ease then stands rather than restarting. The retail hull effects' own declared render states blend only while the unit's light scale alpha is below 1 and bind full opacity otherwise (already read for `MeshBumpColorize.fx`/`RSkinBumpColorize.fx`, `src/presentation/godot/legacy/bump_colorize.hpp`), so a fading unit is genuinely translucent, not merely dimmed. The remake applies the ease as the entity's own geometry transparency (`GodotRenderer::set_unit_opacity`), with transparent environment layers ordered before the foreground blend (FW-19). | research FWE-13, FWE-14; project (the mechanism, and 0.025 read directly from FoC's own comparison) |
| FW-19 | FoC gives each hardpoint attachment its own fog state and model light scale (FWE-13, BP-21). The remake shares one unit opacity across every model piece, including attachments and the shield shell, as a project presentation rule; native shield-shell and particle fade ownership remains unverified. The renderer uses smooth geometry transparency; explicit-alpha mesh shaders multiply their authored alpha by the same instance opacity. Partially faded models stop casting shadows until fully opaque again, preserving assets that never cast shadows; this avoids an opaque ghost silhouette and is a project presentation rule. Environment transparents draw before fading foreground geometry, so nebula layers cannot paint over the hull. Unit particle streams multiply that opacity into their presentation brightness; engines additionally keep BP-45 brightness. Already running streams remain attached until the host finishes fading out. The remake applies one ease to the whole ship because M2 uses one fog test for the whole vessel (V-16). | research (model light scale and attachment states, FWE-13); project (shared opacity for all live unit pieces and streams, geometry transparency and backdrop ordering) |
| FW-20 | The minimap blip does not fade with the model: a separate flag shows it once the ease has risen through 0.75, or hides it once the ease has fallen through 0.25, whichever end it is heading for — a small lag behind the model's own fade, and behind V-16's raw test. The remake's minimap keeps reading V-16's raw test directly (MM-10), unfaded and unlagged (fidelity list): a documented deviation, since M2's HUD has nothing else to keep it in step with. | research FWE-13; project (minimap unchanged) |
| FW-21 | A unit destroyed while its ease has not finished plays its ordinary death sequence at wherever it was last drawn (the light scale's several factors, a death fade included, multiply together rather than one overriding another); one destroyed after it had already faded fully out is not drawn at all and shows nothing, exactly as V-16 already treats a unit the player never saw. The remake's existing rule ("a unit the local player did not see leaves no clone") extends the same way: a unit still above FW-18's hide point when it dies gets its clone at that pose; one already faded below it does not. | research FWE-13 (the light scale's factors multiply); project (extending the existing death-clone rule) |
| FW-22 | unplayable-border fog tiles: FoC has a second, red mode of the same plane, its deployment (reinforcement) overlay. It uses the same fog cells, ring and blur (FW-08 to FW-12), but the colour ramp is `SpaceReinforceFOWColor` (255, 0, 0, 254 in `gameconstants.xml`, ramped to clear like FW-11) and the tile is `W_Space_Reinforce_FOW_Grid` (128 × 128; a soft-edged square of alpha about 66 with a 10-texel clear margin, stronger than the fog's 18, so the tiles read as red blocks). Before the blur, every cell that shows more than a quarter clear (intensity above 64) is asked whether a deployment there is blocked: blocked is fully fogged (red), the rest fully clear. In FoC a point is blocked when it lies within the reinforcement-prevention radius of an object the player is not allied with. Every fogged cell stays red, so with nothing blocking, the unseen space and the outermost, unplayable ring of the map are what draw red. | research FWE-15 (debug build: the overlay's texture path, its sampler and the tile and ramp choice); data (`SpaceReinforceFOWColor`, the tile) |
| FW-23 | unplayable-border fog tiles: FoC shows that mode only while the reinforcement pane is open (or, when a game constant asks for it, only while dragging a reinforcement); otherwise the normal fog of FW-02 to FW-12 draws, and its unplayable ring is the same white tiles as any fogged cell. M2 has no reinforcement pane, so the remake draws the overlay only on request (`--eawr-live-deploy-overlay on`), through `FogField::set_deployment_overlay`, whose blocked-point function is the hook later deployment zones (and reinforcement-blocking objects) supply. | research FWE-15; project (the switch) |
| FW-24 | A space map prop with neither `HIDE_WHEN_FOGGED` nor `TEAM` in `Behavior` or `SpaceBehavior` cannot be fogged. Asteroid fields, impassable asteroids, debris and construct props retain their models and attached particles outside sensor contact; the fog plane still draws with FW-02. The remake classifies session-owned `SpaceProp` placements once from the loaded behavior lists and merges their living IDs into draw visibility, including emitter catch-up samples. Selection, minimap and simulation retain their existing sensor contacts. | research FWE-16 (debug build: the object fog test exits clear before sampling cells when neither behavior is present); data (space prop behavior lists); project (presentation-only admission) |
| FW-25 | `Last_State_Visible_Under_FOW` admits remembered models by effective type, without a station or structure class restriction. `Initial_State_Visible_Under_FOW` seeds the remembered entry as already known; with it false, no copy can appear before first sight. Copy creation also requires a currently drawable source model, so an initially known object already hidden at initialization has knowledge but no mesh copy yet. The last-state tag also makes the live model's opacity jump immediately between full and hidden (WSU-05), while ordinary models keep FW-16. Both flags are validated through inherited unit and obstacle loaders and excluded from replay identity. | research FGE-1, FGE-2 (debug build: entry admission and initial visibility); data; project (presentation storage) |
| FW-26 | After a known model leaves sight, a separate model draws at the saved transform, with the last observed alternate and damage art, and the colour prescribed by FW-30. Its light scale RGB is set to 0.5 and its opacity is full, replacing transient live light-scale effects. It has no collision or selection, no bars, no shield shell and no particle streams. The remake retains the shown mesh uploads and copies the skin palette to independent entity IDs; the transform and palette remain static. | research FGE-3 (debug build: model creation, collision disabled, shield down, alternate copied, half RGB); WSU-06; project (frozen palette and attachments) |
| FW-27 | Destroying an object while its ghost is drawn does not remove or change that ghost. Once any of its saved fog sample points becomes clear, the destroyed object's ghost and remembered entry leave. The remake samples the saved centre, matching its single-point visibility rule V-16, and releases retained uploads and palette state when it forgets the entry. Hidden death plays no death clone. | research FGE-4 (debug build: service of missing objects and saved sample points); project (single point, death-clone admission) |
| FW-28 | Re-seeing a living object removes its ghost immediately and marks it known. Subsequent visible frames refresh the saved appearance; later fog produces a new copy of that state. Changes to position, damage, construction or ownership under fog do not refresh the copy. | research FGE-3, FGE-5 (debug build: reveal transition and one-copy guard); project (capture at the last drawn frame) |
| FW-29 | The remembered scene belongs to the local observer. The debug build keys its entries by object ID and uses the local player when servicing saved fog points; it does not share a global all-player discovery flag. The remake keeps memory in each live view. Radar follows the original model's immediate visible/hidden flag for tagged objects, so the ghost supplies no minimap contact. | research FGE-4, FGE-6 (debug build: local observer and radar thresholds after the immediate opacity update); project (per-view memory) |
| FW-30 | Capture-point ghosts use the neutral faction's RGB and its first matching `Faction_Anim_Subindex` mapping for the IDLE animation, regardless of the last seen owner. Non-capture objects preserve their copied colour and animation state. The remake loads neutral RGB through the faction-colour loader, validates the mapping, and freezes the neutral clip's opening pose. Without a declared mapping it chooses the first available IDLE variant; a missing clip preserves the copied pose. | research FGE-7 (debug build: capture-point exception and first matching faction mapping); data; project (static opening pose and deterministic unspecified variant) |

## Cases

| Case | Staging | Expected |
|---|---|---|
| K-1 | The M2 Coruscant start, Rebel local player, fog on | The Rebel fleet and station sit in clear space; around them faint white tiles fill the rest of the playable square, with a soft edge about one cell wide; the planet and the backdrop show through the tile gaps. |
| K-2 | Rebel ships advance | The clear area follows them: cells ahead clear over 16 frames; cells left behind stay clear for about six seconds after the last sensor leaves them (the linger), then fade over about 0.4 s. |
| K-3 | `--eawr-live-reveal on` | No fog plane. |
| K-4 | fog visibility fades: an enemy ship crosses from fogged space into a held cell, then leaves it again | It eases in as it crosses the edge, approaching full opacity after about 0.9 s, and eases out to the hide point in about 0.7 s; 0.25 s is the characteristic smoothing time, not the complete fade duration; it keeps moving, visibly, throughout both ramps. |
| K-5 | unplayable-border fog tiles: `--eawr-live-deploy-overlay on`, Rebel local player | The plane keeps the fog's shape but every fogged cell and the border of the map draw as soft red tiles; the fleet's clear surroundings stay clear. With the option off, the fog is white as in K-1. |

The contract tests (`tests/presentation/space/fog_field_tests.cpp`) cover FW-01, FW-05 and FW-07
to FW-12, with and without the session's cells; `tests/ui/minimap_tests.cpp` covers the minimap's
fog read from the same cells. `tests/presentation/space/unit_fade_tests.cpp` covers FW-16 to
FW-18 and FW-21 (the ease's timing across visibility flips, spawn-is-instant and forgetting a
destroyed unit); `tests/presentation/space/live_units_tests.cpp` covers `interpolate_units`'
`fading` parameter FW-18 needs. `tests/presentation/renderer/test_live_session.py` stages an
enemy crossing the fog edge and reads the report's `fading` opacities across several ticks to
check the ramp (K-4).

Catch-up evaluates the repeated one-frame transition directly, then at most one fractional step. This bounds presentation work per tracked unit independently of the elapsed interval and preserves FW-16, including a configurable slower ease. The settled-unit recheck cadence remains an explicit deviation pending a paired capture that demonstrates its visibility; the debug-build countdown and 0.0001 settling threshold are confirmed.
