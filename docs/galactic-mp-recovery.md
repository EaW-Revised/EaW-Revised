# Galactic multiplayer checkpoint recovery

GCMP-15 is an engine-owned recovery service over the frozen campaign offer. The
live backend entry is `GalacticLanMatch::request_recovery(source)`. Its future
returns a named refusal or successful publication; callers never borrow the
simulation owner or drive its transport. The returning authenticated dense peer
keeps its original saved logical seat. The source may be a non-host survivor. A fully diagnosed outlier automatically
selects the lowest authenticated member of a strict majority among at least three
active summary voters; healthy majority members never start competing requests.

## Checkpoint and bounded suffix

A new campaign captures one immutable checkpoint at tick zero inside the existing
loading/handover barrier, before the live simulation starts. Selected player saves
reuse their agreed PR2 bytes, including the guest's immutable verified receive
buffer. An absent loading barrier or failed optional capture leaves the match
running and recovery refuses with `checkpoint_unavailable`. A later all-peer player-save capture refreshes the
recovery base, even if the host's asynchronous disk write fails. Recovery performs
no live checkpoint capture and writes no private recovery file.

The source retains completed sealed human/session input and each completed state
hash, with an 8 MiB default suffix budget. Engine input is regenerated by fresh
private mounted services. Exhaustion evicts old rows and recovery returns
`log_too_old` until another agreed player-save capture refreshes the base. This is
a qualification limit: the tick-zero base alone does not provide indefinite
recovery in a long campaign. The byte budget is independent of the 64 MiB
checkpoint-transfer limit.

The source copies a bounded immutable package at a simulation boundary. The
returner restores and replays on one private worker, checks the checkpoint digest,
base state hash, every completed suffix hash, and the final state hash. Cancellation
never joins that worker from the live owner; a cancelled job prevents a second job
from starting until its result is drained. It holds no live owner or transport.

## Admission and cutover

1. The returner requests its retained agreed base. The source checks authenticated
   saved-seat binding, lineage, epoch, anchor hash, and complete retained history.
2. PR2 transfers the checkpoint while healthy survivors continue. Its restore ACK
   is deferred until private restore and initial replay succeed.
3. The source proposes a completed-tick fence. For an inactive returner this is
   64 + D ticks ahead; an already stalled or diagnosed frontier can be fenced at
   its current boundary. A peer that already passed the boundary refuses it.
4. At the fence, survivors finish their existing sealed horizon, stop accepting
   new local intents, and independently compare the complete suffix and future
   sealed batches. The returner replays only the final delta on its private worker.
5. Every active survivor and the authenticated returning peer sends the same
   state/frontier/log proof. A departed saved seat receives one trusted recorded
   `SeatRejoin`; an ordinary human-wire `SeatRejoin` remains forbidden.
6. The source commits the proved frontier. Each survivor prepares a replacement
   core around its existing game; only the returner adopts a fresh game and its
   private services. The live owner callback publishes at the simulation boundary
   and returns no tick. Newly accepted future input cannot be erased by the swap.
7. The cores remain held until publication ACKs and an explicit release. This
   prevents a new stream from sending input into another peer's obsolete core.
   A published peer awaiting release retains ordinary input from independently
   released peers, with an 8 MiB byte limit and a roster/horizon frame limit.
   Normal authenticated receive drains it after release; overflow quarantines
   that participant with `catchup_limit`.

The ordinary D5/WGS-P03/P04 departure service runs before the fence gate and before
replacement connections are armed. Recovery adds no two-peer reconnect grace. A
recorded continue-alone split permanently invalidates recovery in that lineage;
`divergent_epoch` forbids its reconciliation. Two-peer diagnosed hash divergence
also has no authoritative recovery source. A room mesh retains its existing
policy and agreed incident counter when its private replacement core is built.

Before voting, a refusal discards the candidate and releases the old live core.
The source may abort before sending commit. A non-source latches its vote before
the first pair send: after that, cancellation keeps its proved candidate and
fence held for the source's decision, since commit may be in flight elsewhere.
If the decision misses the existing deadline, the uncertain owner quarantines.
After commit, an old/new membership rollback is forbidden. A peer unable to publish
stays out of simulation and closes its authenticated pairs. The source releases
the successfully published subset, quarantining unpublished participants; the new
cores then use their existing ordinary departure agreement for those closed
streams. An owner with an uncertain voted frontier leaves locally at the bounded
cutover deadline rather than advancing an old epoch. No private partial game is
published on a refusal.

## Wire envelopes

All integers are little-endian. The match transport supplies the sender; payloads
never select their own authenticated identity. Limits are checked before staging
allocation. These messages do not enter gameplay, setup, replay or monitor queues.

| Kind | Envelope |
| --- | --- |
| 90..94 | Existing checkpoint manifest/chunks/progress/restore/cancel; explicitly recovery-only instances add the trailer below. |
| 95 | Request: kind, agreed anchor (epoch:u64, completed tick:u64, lineage:32, state:32), saved seat:u32, transfer epoch:u64. Exact length 93. |
| 96 | Suffix chunk: kind, transfer epoch:u64, byte offset:u64, total:u64, at most 64 KiB body. Contiguous only; identical duplicates consume no new storage or progress. |
| 97 | Control: kind, transfer epoch:u64, barrier:u64, source:u32, target:u32, stage:u8, anchor:80, sealed-log SHA-256:32, removed mask:u8. Exact length 139. Stages: staged=0, fence=1, vote=2, commit=3, published=4, release=5. The mask is legal only on release. |
| 98 | Named refusal: kind, transfer epoch:u64, reason:u8, target:u32, committed:u8. Exact length 15. |

A recovery-only kind-90 manifest retains the complete byte-exact original setup
offer and two virtual transfer participants (source=0, returner=1). Its trailer is
marker:u8=1, base tick:u64 (including zero), source saved seat:u32, target saved
seat:u32, and four 32-byte digests: file, base state, initial sealed package, original
offer. The ordinary lobby receiver refuses this trailer and still requires a
selected checkpoint at N > 0. Neither the checkpoint container, campaign setup
payload, ordinary manifest encoding, nor setup-offer wire version changes.

The suffix encoding binds its checkpoint/frontier anchors, file digest, logical
seat table, ordered tick inputs and hashes, and the sealed session frontier:
completed tick, D, permanent split flag, mesh incident counter, inclusive producer
cursors, active/departed peers, and canonical future batches. Private activation
checks each producer sequence across future ticks, preserving inclusive sequence
zero and rejecting repeated sequences before publication.

Credential delivery reuses kinds 80/81 and existing pairwise OS-generated keys and
HMAC stream authentication. The GC-only provider projects dense saved peers onto
the frozen native transport slots separately from the byte-exact offer identity;
this also covers rooms with vacant physical slots. Keys never enter checkpoint,
replay, setup offer, diagnostics or presentation state.

The checkpoint transfer retains its four-chunk per-peer flow window and its
30-second idle / 10-minute whole-transfer deadlines. The suffix has a fixed byte
budget and at most four 64 KiB chunks queued per owner service call. The live
incident has 30-second progress / 10-minute whole deadlines; cutover is additionally
bounded by the existing completeness timeout. Named failures leave the surviving
campaign under its existing departure policy.
